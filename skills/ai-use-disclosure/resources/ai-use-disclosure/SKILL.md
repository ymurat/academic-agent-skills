# AI-use disclosure

Description: Use when an academic needs to write, check or place an AI-use disclosure statement for a paper, thesis, grant proposal or peer review report, or asks whether a given AI use must be disclosed for a specific publisher, funder or YÖK.

## How to work

1. Ask for the venue (journal, conference, publisher, funder, thesis) and the actual uses of AI (tool, version, task, which sections).
2. Find the venue's own instructions for authors first. Venue rules override publisher rules. If you cannot open them, say so.
3. Use the policy table below. Every row was read on 2026-10-31 (Europe/Istanbul). Policies change: before giving a final statement, reopen the URL and say the date you checked. If the page changed, follow the page, not this file.
4. Run the decision flow, fill the template, place it where the venue wants it.
5. Writing rules: lead with the answer, no em dashes, no buzzwords, no padding. Never invent a policy line. If something is not in the table and not on a page you opened, write "confirm against the current policy".
6. Never paste a manuscript under review, a proposal under review, or personal data into an external AI tool while helping. Keep such files on local storage.

## Decision flow

1. Did AI shape the research itself (design, data generation or labelling, code, analysis, figures that carry results)? Yes: describe it in Methods with tool, version, provider, settings, date. Every venue above expects this.
2. Did AI generate or substantially restructure manuscript text, translate it, summarise literature, or suggest references? Yes: write a disclosure statement, placed where the venue says (Elsevier: own section; IEEE: acknowledgments; APA: author note or Method; T&F: AI statement). ACM is the exception: writing help alone needs no disclosure.
3. Was it only spelling, grammar or punctuation? Usually skip (Elsevier, Sage, TÜBİTAK, ACM). IEEE recommends disclosing anyway. APA requires disclosure of editing. When unsure, disclose one line.
4. Images: data plots must come from data through reproducible code. Never use AI to create or alter primary research images. Conceptual diagrams: allowed at Elsevier and T&F with caption disclosure.
5. Peer review or panel work: do not put the manuscript or proposal into any external AI tool. For TÜBİTAK reviews, do not use GenAI at all. If a publisher allows language polish of your own report, disclose it in the report.
6. Never list AI as an author or cite it as the primary source of a fact.

## Templates

English (paper):
> Declaration of generative AI use. During the preparation of this work the author(s) used [TOOL, VERSION, PROVIDER] on [DATE RANGE] to [TASK] in [SECTIONS]. The author(s) reviewed and edited the output and take full responsibility for the content. [If research use: Details of AI use in the research are given in Section X.]

Türkçe (makale, tez):
> Üretken yapay zekâ kullanım beyanı. Bu çalışmanın hazırlanmasında [ARAÇ, SÜRÜM, SAĞLAYICI] [TARİH ARALIĞI] tarihleri arasında [GÖREV] amacıyla [BÖLÜMLER] bölümlerinde kullanılmıştır. Üretilen çıktılar yazar(lar) tarafından gözden geçirilmiş, düzeltilmiş ve doğrulanmıştır; içeriğin tüm sorumluluğu yazar(lar)a aittir. [Araştırmada kullanım varsa: Yapay zekânın yöntemdeki kullanımı Bölüm X'te ayrıntılı olarak açıklanmıştır.]

Peer review (English): During the preparation of this report I used [TOOL] only to [improve language of my own text]. No part of the manuscript was entered into the tool. I take full responsibility for the assessment.

Thesis: put the Turkish statement in the Method chapter per the YÖK rehber, and follow the university's Lisansüstü Eğitim Enstitüsü tez yazım kılavuzu (confirm against the current kılavuz).

## Logging habit

Keep `ai-log.csv` next to the manuscript, one row per session: date, tool, model and version, interface (web, API, local), purpose, sections affected, prompt file path, output file path, what you kept or changed, who checked it. Save prompts and outputs as plain files. Elsevier, APA and Wiley ask authors to keep such records and may request them.
